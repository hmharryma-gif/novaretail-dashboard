THREE KEY INSIGHTS
Scope: All supplied records, January 15-February 20, 2023, with all filters selected.

1. Growth opportunity
Growth and Promising records contribute $12,024.40, or 70.86% of recorded revenue. Electronics is the largest category at $7,089.84 across 16 records (41.78%). We recommend a focused follow-up campaign for suitable customers, testing incremental repeat spend against a holdout group. These amounts show where to test; they do not prove future growth or campaign effectiveness.

2. Early warning signs for retention
The 19 Decline-labeled records contribute $3,649.81 (21.51% of recorded revenue) and average 1.58/5 satisfaction. Seventeen of the 19 are rated 1 or 2. We recommend verifying the associated customer identities and investigating low-rated experiences before targeted service recovery. Track subsequent satisfaction and repeat recorded spend. These signals support a retention review, not a confirmed churn prediction.

3. Commercial and marketing priority
Physical stores generate $9,515.50 versus $7,453.50 online, each from 50 records, but average satisfaction is lower in stores: 3.54 versus 3.88. Stores also account for $2,619.89, or 71.78%, of Decline-labeled recorded revenue. We recommend a store service-recovery pilot, evaluated using satisfaction, repeat spend and margin when cost data becomes available. Product mix and customer differences may explain part of the channel gap.

DATA NOTE
The actual workbook has 100 records and 34 distinct CustomerID values, rather than 99 verified consumers. Transaction IDs are reused and customer attributes conflict. We retained all records and report summed PurchaseAmount as recorded revenue. The missing segment remains Unknown, preserving its $249.99. Segment analysis uses record labels and does not assume exclusive customer groups.
